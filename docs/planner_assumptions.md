# Crew planning assumptions and model

The model uses the supplied monthly **forecast** of station-inventory rebalancing proxy demand, never 2026 outcomes. Initial January full-time crews are Boston 6, Cambridge 4, Somerville 1, Brookline 1 and two shared among all other cities. January hires, terminations and seasonal contracts are prohibited because no December 2025 decision is assumed. A decision in month t changes service in t+1. Existing January crews are assumed full-time.

Capacity per crew per month is `120 bikes/shift × 6 shifts/week × calendar days/7`. This prorates fractional weeks; it is aggregate capacity, without daily schedules, travel times, routing, maintenance or loading limits. A bike delivered and a bike collected each consume one counted handling unit if that is how the demand proxy counts them. Relocation routes and two-stop efficiency are not modeled. Changing proxy counting conventions requires changing capacity consistently.

Full-time cost is $6,000 per active month ($54,000/12 driver plus $1,500 van). A firing notice in t removes the crew in t+1 and incurs $13,500 severance then; month t salary remains paid. Hiring is available in the following month and has no separately specified fee. Monthly salary accrual is assumed: the stated annual driver salary is not an annual fixed-term contract. Seasonal crews cost $7,800 per active month, start the following month, remain at least three months, and have no termination fee. The system has at most eight active seasonal crews, including the shared pool. Renewals can last beyond three months. Monthly shifts are not moved between months.

The default shared pool is an explicit interpretation of the conflicting instructions that the other cities share two crews while crews normally operate within a city. All non-core cities form one staffing group; their monthly forecast-proportional capacity allocation is exported separately for every city. Its fractional values are **capacity shares, not dedicated crews**; group crew counts must not be summed again across member cities. This monthly allocation permits within-month sharing and has no route feasibility guarantee. Set `pool_other_cities=False` to apply dedicated-city constraints. The two initial other-city crews then go to forecast-highest-demand other cities, or an explicit `dedicated_other_initial` mapping. This policy requires human review.

The mixed-integer model minimizes 2026 crew salaries and severance, mandatory 2027 seasonal obligations from late-year starts, and an assumed service-loss penalty. Its constraints enforce staffing flow balance, lead times, integer crews, seasonal contract ages and the system cap. Unserved rebalancing proxy demand is `max(0, monthly proxy - monthly capacity)`; monthly surplus capacity cannot satisfy a different month or staffing group. Seasonal starts in November accrue one additional month and December starts two additional months of $7,800 costs in 2027. Their active January/February crews are therefore covered by December's cap (the active subset can only shrink).

No automatic year-end full-time firing or company closure is assumed. Continuing December full-time payroll is reported as a January 2027 monthly run rate, separately from the finite 2026 analysis horizon. The model does not claim to estimate total future lifetime employment costs. This remaining horizon effect deserves review before interpreting late-year hires. Seasonal mandatory tails are charged even when the corresponding 2027 service benefit is outside the evaluation period.

The course's expected loss per **affected user** is `$0.40×0 + 0.45×3.25 + 0.15×(3.25+25) = $5.70`. Proxy bikes are not affected users. `affected_users_per_proxy_bike=1` is a transparent scenario parameter, not an empirical measurement or a factual loss estimate. The objective uses `unserved proxy × assumed affected users per proxy × $5.70`; reports retain crew costs, unserved proxy bikes and assumed dollar losses separately. Evaluate ratios 0, 0.5, 1 and 2, along with demand -20% and +20%. An optimization result is conditional on these assumptions; solver status, gap and time limit are reported, and a feasible time-limited incumbent must not be called proven optimal.

`evaluate_plan` joins available actual months to a frozen plan and never refits staffing. `allocate_city_capacity` conserves shared group crew-equivalent capacity and allocated operating costs. It does not allocate terminal contract obligations to cities; those remain in the staffing summary to avoid duplicate accounting.
